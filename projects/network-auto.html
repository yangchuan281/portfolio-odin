01 context

The only question that matters after an outage.

Two lab routers, edited by hand over SSH. When something stopped routing there was no way to answer the only question that matters: what changed since the last time it worked?

So the console does one thing first: it logs in, captures the running config, and stores it as a version next to the timestamp and the device. Everything else — the diff view, the topology map, the CPU chart — is built on that snapshot.

02 design

Synchronous, boring, and the right size.

Synchronous SSH and SQLite instead of a broker and a task queue. For two to twenty devices that is the right size, and the limits are written down instead of being discovered in production.

03 what is still broken

Two parser gaps I left documented.

Backups are useless if you cannot read them, so configs get diffed with difflib. Two parser gaps are still open and documented: the LLDP branch loses the local interface because the split consumes its label, and CDP drops neighbours that advertise no management address.

I chose to leave both visible rather than paper over them: on the topology page a missing local port reads as —, and a CDP neighbour without a management address is counted but not drawn. A map that quietly lies is worse than a map with a hole in it.

Known limits — stated, not discovered later

  • The LLDP parser splits on "Local Intf:" and consumes the label, so local_port is always empty for LLDP edges.
  • CDP neighbours that advertise no management address are dropped from the graph.
  • SSH is synchronous: a slow device blocks the request that is capturing from it. Fine for a lab, wrong for a thousand devices.
  • Verified against two VyOS routers and vendor CLI references — not against a Cisco or Huawei chassis on this desk.
