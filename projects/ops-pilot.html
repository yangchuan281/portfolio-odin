01 context

A deploy at night, and no way back.

A bad deploy meant SSHing in at night and hoping the previous image tag was remembered correctly. Alerts arrived with no asset context, no owner, and the recovery steps lived in someone's head.

The brief I set myself was narrow on purpose: keep Prometheus, keep Kubernetes, and add only what was missing around them — an approval chain that cannot be skipped, an audit row for every state change, and a rollback that does not need a human awake.

02 design

Wrap the tools, don't replace them.

Don't rebuild Prometheus or Kubernetes. Wrap them: ingest Alertmanager webhooks, add the approval chain, the audit trail and the rollback that were missing. A fake K8s driver keeps the whole flow runnable offline.

03 what broke

I shipped a broken image on purpose.

Drill DR-01 shipped a deliberately broken image: the health gate caught unready replicas and a restart limit, then rolled back in 15.8s. The drills also exposed three real defects — a rollback that could loop onto another bad image, a dangerous action that could never be confirmed, and a dry-run reported as failed.

All three were fixed rather than written around. The rollback now records the image it came from before switching, the approval token is checked before the action executes instead of after, and dry-run results are stored with their own status so they can no longer be mistaken for a failure.

Scope boundaries — what this is not

  • Single-node Docker Compose, not a highly available cluster. It is a control plane for a lab, sized like one.
  • A check that overruns its own interval is skipped, not queued — the lock does not build a backlog.
  • The Kubernetes driver covers Deployment image updates, rollout status, pods, events and rollback. It does not apply arbitrary manifests.
  • Alert ingestion expects the Alertmanager webhook shape; a different sender needs a small adapter.
