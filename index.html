01 $ whoami

Reliability is a feature.
I'm learning to build it in.

Not ten years of enterprise delivery — a few systems I built end to end, including the drills where I broke them on purpose to see what happened.

I study network engineering. Most of what I know about running systems came from building one: a FastAPI control plane for assets, checks, alerts and releases, a Flask console that talks to real routers over SSH, a WeCom service desk that turns a question into a ticket.

The work I care about is the unglamorous part — an approval chain that cannot be skipped, a rollback that fires without a human, a diff that tells me what changed since yesterday. Documentation is part of the deliverable: every number on this page has a command behind it.

  1. 01

    Prove it with a failure

    A feature that has never failed in front of me is a feature I do not understand. Four drills, injected on purpose, evidence kept.

  2. 02

    Automate the second time

    First time is learning. Second time is a script. Third time is a pipeline that nobody has to think about.

  3. 03

    Write it down

    A system nobody can explain is a system nobody can hand over. Runbooks are cheaper than archaeology.
